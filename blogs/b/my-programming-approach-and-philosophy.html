My Programming Approach and Philosophy

Braden Wood Apr 23, 2025

There's a suprising philosophy behind how I code, and its shaped by one idea: "building software is never a one-and-done process"-Said as I was being interviewed.

I've been coding for over 4 years, self-taught, switching from language to language like Javascript, Tailwind, PHP, and even less-than-nice ones like C++ or BrainF**k, not because I wanted to be flashy and sway around these big heavy languages like a medal, but because I wanted to understand. That idea has shaped how I build projects today.

My process is usually like this:

  1. 1. Build.
  2. 2. Refine.
  3. 3. Rebuild.

Build

Let`s start with what is, for me, essentially the hardest step of the whole process. I call this the thinking phase.

Unless theres a customer with a clear request, I have to rely on my imagination, which, honestly, is the slowest part of my brain. Im not naturally imaginative. Coming up with an idea can take forever. Its frustrating, its slow, and it feels like dragging inspiration out of thin air.

But eventually, something clicks. An idea forms. Thats when I move into action getting the idea out of my head and into code. Its rough. Its messy. Sometimes its just straight-up ugly. But it works. It proves the concept, reveals flaws, and shows me what the real challenges are.

At this stage, Im not aiming for perfection. Im aiming for clarity.

Refine

This step is quite easy, I revisit what I've built and start cleaning up. This is where I care about usability, readability, and structure. It's about making it not just work, but work well for both the end user and the next developer (even if that's me, six months from now).

Rebuild

Finally, if the project has long-term potential, I rebuild. This is the step where performance matters. I revisit my assumptions, rethink architecture, and sometimes throw out everything I wrote in step one. Because if it's worth keeping, it's worth doing right. This rebuild phase isn't failure; it's evolution. It's the mark of a project that grew from a sketch into something real.

Some people write code to show off. I write code to grow; to grow skills, grow ideas, and grow systems that stand the test of time. Or what most people would call a "well made website".
